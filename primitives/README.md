# Nonogram Primitives

User interface experiments dressed up as games: a nonogram that zooms out each time a board is solved, served at `/games/notan-zoom/`. A rough prototype. Player feedback and the open items from it are in `docs/primitives-feedback-20261104.md`. It is developed and deployed from game-riffs (`notan-zoom-lab/`); the shareable copy is the `primitives/` folder of the private `lukeslp/notan` repo, synced by `tools/publish-notan-source.sh`. Six tabs, each its own game. Every chain starts small and easy and ramps up a band at a time. Not linked from anywhere, `noindex`, no control frame yet, saves under `notan-zoom-lab.v1`.

**Nonogram Journey** (`journey.html`, `journey.js`, `journey.css`, `levels.js`) is the rework. It plays Notan's own hand-drawn ramp (`levels.js` is a copy of `notan/levels.js`), 2×2 up to 15×15, and makes every fifth board a twist, in turn: Merge (into a mirrored board, like Notan's endless ones), Patch, Gravity (which replaces Pixel) and Mines on a hand-drawn picture, starting at boards 5, 10, 15 and 20. Every board is fixed, so the level list in its menu sheet opens any of them; after board 58 come Notan's endless boards. Gravity, Ring, Hole and Mines are also games of their own in that sheet. Design and history in `docs/nonogram-journey-20261104.md`. Its turns are slower than the lab's and nothing but Mines is dramatic: a solved picture holds 2 s, plain boards crossfade in place, a camera move takes 2.6 s. Preview at `/games/notan-zoom/journey.html`, noindex, saves under `notan-journey.v3`.

- **Merge.** Starts 5×5, then 6×6, 6×6, 7×7, and 8×8 from the fifth board on. On a win every 2×2 block merges into one cell (ink when more than half of it is ink, a coin toss at a tie; at an odd edge a block is cut short). The seed, half the old board's side rounded up, lands off center, away from where the old picture's ink leaned, so the picture keeps growing into open space instead of filling a frame. It is never dead center. The old board lands on its seed two squares to one.
- **Pixel.** Starts 3×3, then 5×5. On a win the whole board becomes the middle cell of the next 5×5 and counts as filled. The cell is drawn like any locked filled square (gray, or ink when a run of your ink joins it) with the shrunken picture pressed in faintly, nested.
- **Hole.** Starts 5×5 with one locked empty square in the middle. On a win the whole board falls into the hole: every ink square outside it adds one to the mass, and the hole's area grows by a twelfth of a square per unit of mass. The hole is a void (its squares are locked empty), so a line through it splits in two. The board keeps two squares round the hole, so it grows 5, 7, 9, 11, 13. Past 13×13 the hole collapses: each square now holds three times the mass, and the board shrinks back. New pictures swirl in toward it in two or three arms, always the same way round in one chain, denser near the edge. The void is drawn as a round well under the board, darkest at the middle, with faint dust on spiral arms that turns slowly the chain's way round and thickens with mass; its own squares draw nothing. Squares near the well lean toward it, shrink a little and turn with its spin, more the closer they are, in a slow wave that circles the hole; only the drawing moves, so each square keeps its place for taps. With reduced motion the well and the squares hold still in the leaning pose.
- **Ring.** Starts 3×3. Each win adds a ring around the old board, two or three squares across for rings 1 to 3 (one on each side, and an odd one on the side the ring leans toward), up to four for rings 4 and 5, then up to six. Short of a merge, a ring is never more than about 1.6 times the open squares of the board before (or 24 more), so in practice a ring adds at most one square across more than the last one did, four comes only right after a merge, and boards run 3, then 5 or 6, then 7 to 9, and so on to 13. The lean wanders a little each time, so the core drifts off center like a tree's from the first ring on. Dense and sparse rings tend to alternate, and the readout names each one. When a ring would pass 13×13 the board merges 2×2 and grows on.
- **Patch.** One 15×15 picture cut into nine 5×5 tiles, laid flat as a plate. The middle tile is a window onto your last picture, a layer below, whose own middle is a window onto the one before (up to four layers). With a mouse over the plate, those layers shift a few pixels against the pointer, more the deeper they are; nothing moves on touch or with reduced motion. The other eight tiles are small boards. The four edge tiles start with the edge they share with the old picture locked, so it runs on into them, and solving a tile locks the edge it shares with each open neighbor (which can finish one outright), so the order you pick matters. Open a tile and the camera dives in; solve it and the camera pulls back. Solve all eight and the camera pulls back by three, level, so the whole picture becomes the first layer under the next plate.
- **Mines.** A nonogram where filling a square that should stay empty hits a mine. It zooms like Merge, 5×5, 6×6, 7×7, then 8×8, on the same off-center seed, well and camera. Only fills are checked; marks never are, like a Minesweeper flag, so a wrong mark costs nothing now but can mislead you later. A found mine goes off once (a single flash, shards, amber sparks, a puff of smoke and a small shake of the board), then locks its square empty, scorched, with a cross in a dashed square; with reduced motion only the scorched square appears, and forced colors draw no effect. A heart in the bar, shown on the Mines tab only, sets how many mines lose a board: Relaxed (an outline) 5, Careful (half full, the default) 3, Perfect (full and cracked) 1. Each press moves to the next and is saved. The new rule applies to the board in play unless that board has already hit as many mines as the new limit, in which case it starts with the next board, so changing the rule never loses a board on the spot. Each board keeps its own limit. Reaching the limit loses that board only: the answer squares you had not filled are outlined, and Another board deals a new picture at the same zoom, size and locked squares (it waits 600 ms after the loss and ignores held keys, so the reveal cannot be skipped). On a pointer a pick is previewed while you hold and happens only when you let go. A drag starts only once the pointer is half a square (at least 10 px) from where it went down, so a tap that wobbles stays one square; it follows the main direction of the move while it is one or two squares long, then keeps to that row or column, stops at its first mine (one mine per drag at most), skips squares you marked empty, and letting go more than a square off the board cancels it. Only the finger that started a pick moves or commits it; a second finger is ignored. A hold of 300 ms turns a fill into a mark and never the other way, so in Marking mode a hold stays a mark: a fill comes only from a tap or drag in Filling mode, a right-click or a key. A fill on a square you marked empty clears the mark instead, the way a flag guards a square. Keyboard picks happen at once. Undo and Clear board never take back a mine: one Undo takes back the fills and marks before it, and the mine stays. Found mines, the count and a lost board survive a reload. Every Mines board passes the strictest gate: the simple rules below finish it in at most four rounds (five from 7×7), at least 40% of its open squares (and two ink squares) open in the first round, every round leaves at least two lines to work on with no bottleneck, and no clue is longer than three numbers. The first 5×5 keeps the easiest of three such boards and later boards the middle one. Line logic only gains from what you learn, so a careful player always has a square the numbers prove and never has to risk a mine.

Pictures come from five generators: blobs (noise smoothed by majority passes), strokes (lines that wander out of the old picture's ink), terrain (a ground line seen from a random side, with an island or a cave; Merge and Mines only, since on a 15×15 Patch picture it leaves whole tiles blank or solid), scatter (small clumps) and the hole's swirl. The first two boards of a chain are blobs; after that no generator is used twice running. Locked cells always keep their value, so ink grows out of the old picture. As in a plain nonogram and in Notan, nothing else starts locked: only the squares a mode carries over (Merge's and Mines' seed, Pixel's middle square, Hole's void, Ring's core, Patch's shared edges).

Every board is solved from its clues and those carried-over squares alone, with the rules a person uses, not only by Notan's line solver (`engine.js`, a copy of `notan/engine.js`), which tries every way a line can fit and so finishes boards that leave a person stuck. Two rule sets grade boards. The simple rules are a line with no numbers or with all its ink down, an exact count, gaps too small for any block, the overlap of each block slid left and right against the marks, and ink glued to an edge. Packing adds the overlap of the leftmost and rightmost packings that respect ink as well as marks. When the band's rules stall on a new picture, one of the squares they could not settle is flipped and they try again, the way Notan's own generator does; Notan's line solver must then finish the board too, which proves it has one answer. Like Notan, it turns down boards that are too sparse or dense, have more than one empty line, or mirror themselves.

Each board must fall in its band, set by how many boards into the chain it is:

| Board | Rules that must finish it | Rounds at most | Lines moving every round | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | simple | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| 2nd, 3rd | simple | 4 | 2 | 3 |
| 4th | simple | 5 | 2 | 4 |
| 5th | simple | 6 | 2 | 4 |
| 6th, 7th | packing | 7 | 2 | 5 |
| 8th on | packing | 8 | 2 | 6 |

A round is one sweep of every row and column against what the last sweep knew. Requiring two or more lines to move every round means there is always more than one place to look. Pixel asks three lines through its fifth board and aims no higher than four rounds, since its board never grows. Hole climbs the table a row a board, but never switches to packing on a board that just grew, since a bigger board and new rules at once is a step (its sixth board is usually the first 9×9, so packing waits a board). Patch tiles must be finished by the simple rules in four rounds from their locked edge; a tile that is not counts as poor, and the plate tries other pictures. A poor tile left after twenty tries (about one plate in two hundred) gets a picture of its own that keeps its locked edge. If no picture settles at all, for a board or a tile (never seen in sampling), the open squares of every other row are filled and the rest left empty around the locked squares; each row then finishes on its own, so even that board has one answer and needs no guess. Mines has its own stricter gate (above). Of up to three boards in the band, it keeps the one nearest the band's aim, ties going to the lower Notan score. It used to keep the one with the highest Notan score, which rewards exactly what makes a board feel stuck. Patch tiles are nudged the same way and written back into the big picture.

Every change of board is one camera move. The view on screen is lifted out and held in place, the next view is laid out at its resting size, and both travel together so the old board always lies exactly on its place in the new one (or, zooming into a Patch tile, the tile on the board it opens into). The old board's numbers fade first and its picture melts into the locked squares as it lands. The timing is calm on purpose (a player found the old one jarring): a solved board holds still for 1.8 s so you can look at it, the camera then moves for 2.2 s on an even ease-in-out curve (`cubic-bezier(.45,0,.55,1)`), and the landing is one 0.4 s crossfade, the old picture out and the whole new board in. The new board is generated and built before the move and the move starts two frames later from its first pose, so nothing heavy runs while it moves; the new board stays hidden while the old one flies, nothing animates per square, and shadows are dropped in flight. Tests run shorter timings through `window.__zoomTiming`, and `test-play.cjs` checks that the shipped defaults stay calm. There are no grid lines: every square is a tile inset from its cell. Open squares are faint tiles, your ink is raised with a shadow, a square marked empty sinks, and the carried-over picture sits gray and pressed in a recessed well, one layer below; squares you just laid land raised at full ink and then sink into it. Groups of five get a wider seam. The Patch picture draws its tiles the same way, so a tile lines up square for square when you zoom into it. Gray squares are locked and count as filled; a gray square in an unbroken row or column run that your own ink joins turns ink-colored with a thin gray inner ring, so the run reads as one block. In dark mode ink squares have a crisp edge rather than a glow, and the well is a step lighter than the page. Locked empty squares are bare well (only forced colors gives them a dash). When a new board comes in, screen readers hear what it carries over (for example "Carried over and locked: 5 filled, 4 empty"). Pixel's locked cell is filled gray with the last board pressed into it, nesting older boards the same way.

The bar is icons only; each button keeps its label as its accessible name and has a tooltip, and the icons say how far each reaches. Undo (a hooked arrow) takes back your last move (Z). Filling and Marking are one toggle icon (a filled square or a dot) and say what a tap does; right-click and a hold do the other (in Mines a hold only marks). Clear board clears your marks on this board (on the Patch plate, Clear tiles clears every open tile); Undo brings them back, on the plate only while you stay there, since opening a tile starts a fresh undo. All tiles (Patch only) goes back to the plate. Clear board is one board with a restart arrow; New game (boards nested inside each other, the whole sequence, with a plus) starts this tab over with new pictures; if you have progress, it first asks in the bar and says what goes ("This one ends at zoom 3." or "Your marks on this board will be cleared."), with Keep playing focused and drawn stronger and New game plain. Keep playing takes New game's place, or the row slides (on a narrow bar the two choices trade places) so the confirm never sits over the spot you just pressed, and a press on it within 600 ms is ignored. The question never times out; Escape, a tab or a touch on the board keeps the game. Theme switches light and dark. Undo and Clear board stay in place when they would do nothing, marked unavailable, and say why if pressed, on screen in the line under the bar as well as to screen readers. Arrows announce each square ("Row 3, column 5, open"). A drag paints every square it passes along its row or column, however fast, and only the finger that started it paints. The line under the board is a short key hint ("Tap fills · hold marks"), and the board names its keys to screen readers (`aria-keyshortcuts`); six tabs fit one row down to 300 px. A tab pressed during a zoom opens when the zoom ends. Left and right arrows move along the tabs; on any button only Z reaches the board. Keyboard focus stays with the board through a zoom (on the Patch plate, the first open tile), and a new plate is announced. The board and the Patch tiles draw no focus ring by default; the **Focus outline** checkbox at the end of the hint line turns one on, and the choice is saved. Without it, a keyboard that brings focus to the board shows the square cursor at once, a focused Patch tile lifts as it does under a mouse, and forced colors still outline a focused tile. In forced colors (Windows High Contrast) the board draws itself in system colors: open squares outlined, your ink solid, a mark a dot, a locked filled square hatched with a double edge, a locked empty square a dash, a found mine a dashed square with a cross.

`BASE=http://127.0.0.1:8765 node notan-zoom-lab/test-gen.cjs` builds six chains of twelve boards in every mode without clicking. In the page it checks that the line solver finishes every board from its clues and carried-over squares alone, that nothing else starts locked, the start sizes and each mode's ramp, and each mode's rules (off-center Merge and Mines seeds, ring widths and that at least a quarter of the rings before a merge are lopsided, Hole margins and its band waiting a board after a size step, Patch edges). For Mines it also checks the gate, that a careful player who fills only what one line proves never hits a mine, the pick rules (a run stops at its first mine, marks are never checked, a lost board takes no picks) and Another board. Then, in node, `test-solve.cjs`, which shares no code with the page, counts every board's answers by backtracking over complete line logic (exactly one, and the page's), checks every board against its band with its own copy of the human rules, checks the Mines gate, checks that no board has much more open ground than the one before it (at most 1.75 times, or 24 more, short of a merge or collapse), and checks that each depth averages within half a round of its aim, which fails if generation goes back to keeping the hardest board. It brute-forces a few thousand lines and small boards first to check the checker.

`node notan-zoom-lab/test-solvable.cjs [--chains N]` needs no browser or server. It runs the generation code of `zoom.js` and `journey.js` in node and checks with `test-solve.cjs` that every board has exactly one answer from its clues and locked squares, that it is the stored picture (the page wins, and Mines places its mines, by the stored picture, so a second answer would be refused), that line logic finishes it from its locked squares, with single-square contradiction probing if it stalls, and that every locked square agrees with the picture. It covers the Journey's 58 fixed boards, N chains of twelve in every mode (40 by default), Another board at every Mines zoom, every Patch tile, the fallback picture, and a run in which no picture ever settles. An audit of 20,000 chains per mode on 2026-11-16 found no board that failed.

`BASE=http://127.0.0.1:8765 node notan-zoom-lab/test-play.cjs <shots-dir>` plays each tab through a few zooms at desktop and phone size and reports page errors. Partway through each zoom out of a board it also checks that every square on the departing board is still in its own row and column, so a change that drops or shifts squares mid-zoom fails the run (exit code 1). It then checks that New game asks before throwing away progress (and not when there is nothing to lose), that Escape, a tab or a touch on the board keeps the game and a quick second tap does nothing; that the confirm keeps clear of the New game button (Merge and a Patch tile) and is drawn plain beside a stronger Keep playing; plays a scripted Mines board at 390 and 320 wide (a wrong fill, Undo saying so on screen and Clear board keeping the mine, a drag that stops at its mine and keeps to its row, one Undo taking back the fills before a mine, a tap that wobbles over an edge, a second finger, canceling by letting go off the board, a hold that marks, a hold in Marking mode, a fill on a mark and a fill drag across marks, the lost board and Another board, a reload, both themes, one flash per mine that clears away); checks the rules heart (Mines only, Careful by default, cycling Perfect 1, Relaxed 5, Careful 3 and saved, and a stricter rule waiting for the next board rather than losing the one in play) and that reduced motion hits a mine without the effect; checks that six tabs and every bar control fit at 320, 375, 390 and 1200 wide, icons only, each with a hidden name and a tooltip; checks keyboard focus through a zoom and onto the Patch plate, a tab pressed mid-zoom, keys on a tab, a fast drag, and that the board clears the bar at 844×390; and checks that forced colors still draw every kind of square. Set `CHROMIUM_PATH` to use a browser already on disk when Playwright's own build is missing.

On phones the page does not zoom: `html, body { touch-action: pan-x pan-y; }` stops double-tap and pinch zoom, the viewport sets `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=no` for Android, and `zoom.js` cancels iOS Safari's gesture events and two-finger moves. Unlike the other games (`docs/double-tap-zoom-20261011.md`), pinch is off here on purpose, because a pinch could leave the board filling the screen with no way back. After changing `zoom.js`, `zoom.css` or `engine.js`, run `tools/stamp-assets.py notan-zoom-lab` so phones don't pair the page with a cached script.

Patch's 3D is CSS only (perspective, preserve-3d). Each layer is drawn exactly as its plate looked, a third the size, at three times the resolution, so the pull-back between pictures is one exact 2D zoom with nothing swapped. The plate no longer tilts (the tilted plate with shaft walls was dropped on 2026-11-03), so the walls stay hidden. The plate's base and tiles share one flat plane, because a separate base plane half a pixel lower lost depth ties mid-zoom and blanked tiles, and swallowed clicks on the lower row.
